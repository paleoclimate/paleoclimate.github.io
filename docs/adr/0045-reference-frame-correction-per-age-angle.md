# Reference-frame correction: one angle per age

`PALEO_REFERENCE_FRAME_CORRECTIONS` rotated every age by the same Euler rotation, pole (0° N, 60° E) and −13°, after the GPlates reconstruction and before the IDW. It entered in `06b32b7` ("feat: Release v1.0.1", 2026-05-08 09:01 −03:00), one hour after `fd2408b` switched the map from Web Mercator to equirectangular (2026-05-08 08:00). Its comment says it was "calibrated empirically against the 100 Ma reference" so that the equator crosses northern Brazil at 100 Ma, as in published paleogeographic atlases. It was a visual fit, at one age, against the fixed 30° graticule; the value never changed, and no ADR recorded it.

The correction does not fix a projection or datum error. The Mercator misalignment was already solved by `fd2408b`, and it separated the raster from the vectors, while this rotation moves points, anchors, coastlines and basin outlines together. The exported point files are a pure rigid rotation of the present-day coordinates (median residual 0.000° to 0.057° per age), with no swapped lon/lat, 0–360° longitudes or datum shift, and the geodetic-geocentric difference is at most 0.19°. What the rotation does is change the reference frame: from the mantle frame of the plate model (Zahirovic et al., 2022, OptimisedMantleRef) towards a paleomagnetic one. A mantle frame does not record true polar wander, and using one in place of a paleomagnetic frame can put a site more than 15° off in paleolatitude (van Hinsbergen et al., 2015); here the model frame puts the Brazilian basins 11.7° to 20.9° south of the paleomagnetic position between 95 and 145 Ma.

**Evaluation of the constant.** The area centroids of seven Brazilian margin basins (Foz do Amazonas, Pará-Maranhão, Barreirinhas, Potiguar, Sergipe-Alagoas, Campos, Santos; plate 201) were compared, at the 17 ages, with the paleolatitude of the same places in the global APWP of Vaes et al. (2023), queried through paleolatitude.org; Torsvik et al. (2012) was the second reference. Only the seven present-day basin centroids, rounded to 0.01°, were sent to the site. Against Vaes et al., the −13° constant is within 1.6° from 95 to 110 Ma, leaves 2.8° to 8.2° from 115 to 145 Ma, and makes 65 to 90 Ma worse: 4.5° to 14.6° north of the reference, where the model frame alone was 0.3° to 8.5° off. The angle that zeroes the error moves with age, from +1.6° at 65 Ma to −21.4° at 140 and 145 Ma.

**Considered options:** (1) one angle per age about the same pole, fitted to Vaes et al. (2023); (2) export every age again from GPlates with anchor plate 701701, the paleomagnetic frame shipped with the Zahirovic et al. (2022) model, and drop the correction; (3) keep the constant. (3) is wrong outside 95–110 Ma. (2) is the cleanest, since every plate would sit in a documented paleomagnetic frame, but it needs a new export of all the GeoJSON files and has not been tried. (1) is a table in the existing dictionary, and with the fitted angle the RMS error of the seven basins falls to 0.03°–0.34° at every age.

**Decision:** (1). Each age `'65_ma'` … `'145_ma'` gets its own entry `(0.0, 60.0, angle)`, with the angle from the `angulo_otimo_vaes` column of the evaluation (scan from −30° to +10° in 0.1° steps, minimizing the RMS paleolatitude error of the seven basins). 140 and 145 Ma share −21.4°. The `'default'` entry stays at the previous −13° only as a fallback for a dataset name outside the table, so nothing that used to be rotated silently stops being rotated.

**Limitations:** the fit uses seven points, all on plate 201 and on the Brazilian margin, and keeps the pole fixed at (0°, 60° E), so it is valid for South America only; Africa, the Caribbean and anything far from the pole may be off by a different amount. The reference APWP has its own 2° to 4° uncertainty in paleolatitude, and Vaes et al. and Torsvik et al. differ by up to 4° between 95 and 145 Ma. It is an empirical fit and must be described as one.

**Consequences:** Every published map, raster, PDF and comparison changes position against the graticule, and the class pattern changes a little, because the IDW measures distance in degrees of the rotated frame. The validation and the atlas comparisons have to be run again after regenerating. Option (2), the paleomagnetic export with anchor 701701, stays as future work; if it is done, the table goes back to no correction.
