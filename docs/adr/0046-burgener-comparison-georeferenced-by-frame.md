# Burgener palaeo-Köppen maps are a third comparison, georeferenced by their frame

Scotese et al. (2024, *The Cretaceous world*, GSL Special Publications 544, doi:10.1144/SP544-2024-28, CC BY 4.0) reprint the palaeo-Köppen maps of Burgener et al. (2023) for nine ages that the atlas also maps: 145, 130, 120, 105, 95, 90, 85, 80 and 70 Ma (Figs. 89–97). `compare_burgener.py` scores each against the class raster the viewer paints, with the same confusion matrix, accuracy, kappa and per-class IoU as the Floegel comparison. The figures are stored as `COMPARISON/inputs/{age}_burgener.jpg`, the JPEG embedded in the PDF, unchanged.

The figures are global equirectangular plates (180° W–180° E, 90° S–90° N) inside a black frame, so the frame is the georeference and no control points are picked. Their palaeogeography is PALEOMAP; ours is the GPlates model with the per-age frame correction (`docs/adr/0045-reference-frame-correction-per-age-angle.md`). The continents therefore sit several degrees apart. A rotation and a shift in degrees, fitted by the symmetric truncated chamfer distance between the Burgener shoreline and our coastline inside the raster window, takes up that offset, as the hand-picked points do for Floegel. Powell searches ±20° of rotation and ±15° of shift from 27 starts; a fit that stops at the limit is an error. The headline score is frame plus fit; the frame-only score is reported beside it.

Class mapping, from the standard Köppen B group: BWh and BWk are Dry; BSh and BSk are Semi-arid; Af/Am, As/Aw, Ca, Cb, Cc, Da, Db and Dc/Do are Humid. Sea, frame, labels and graticule are ignored. Pixels take the nearest legend color in Lab (Fig. 2c swatches), then a 5×5 majority vote, because a Burgener cell is about 4 px and the graticule crosses the cells. A sensitivity score also counts As/Aw (seasonally dry tropics) as Semi-arid. Only cells where Burgener has land are compared, as with Floegel.

**Considered options:** (1) frame georeference plus a fitted rigid offset; (2) frame georeference only; (3) hand-picked control points per age, as for Floegel. (2) mostly measures the difference between the two plate models. (3) is nine more manual sessions and is not reproducible by the scorer. (1) is automatic and reports (2) beside it.

**Limitations:** one rotation and shift per age covers the whole window, so the relative position of South America and Africa between the two models is not corrected. The shoreline fit compares a palaeo-shoreline (Burgener) with present-day coastlines rotated to the age (ours). The Köppen–three-class mapping is a choice: our Semi-arid class is wider than Köppen BS, which is why As/Aw is scored both ways.

**Consequences:** the viewer gets a "Comparison Burgener (2023)" button, shown for the nine ages with a report; the Floegel button is renamed "Comparison Hay & Flögel (2012)" (Hay & Flögel, 2012, Earth-Science Reviews) so both name the source and year; and `index_comparison.html` lists the Burgener reports. Publishing the site runs `compare_burgener.py compare` after the Floegel and Hay steps. `COMPARISON/burgener_summary.csv` holds one row per age.
